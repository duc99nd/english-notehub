# Family Members

## Why family words first

Almost every English conversation starts with "How are you?" and then goes straight to family: "Do you have siblings?" "Are you married?" "How old are your parents?" Knowing 12 family words unlocks half of small-talk English.

## Core family words

| English               | Vietnamese            | Notes                                          |
| --------------------- | --------------------- | ---------------------------------------------- |
| mother / mom / mummy  | mẹ                    | "Mom" is informal, "mother" is more formal     |
| father / dad / daddy  | bố / cha / ba         | "Dad" is informal, "father" is more formal     |
| sister                | chị / em gái          | English doesn't distinguish older/younger      |
| brother               | anh / em trai         | same — no age distinction                      |
| daughter              | con gái (của cha mẹ)  | someone's daughter                             |
| son                   | con trai (của cha mẹ) | someone's son                                  |
| grandmother / grandma | bà                    | mother of your parent                          |
| grandfather / grandpa | ông                   | father of your parent                          |
| aunt                  | cô / dì / bác gái     | parent's sister or wife of parent's brother    |
| uncle                 | chú / bác / cậu       | parent's brother or husband of parent's sister |
| cousin                | anh chị em họ         | child of your aunt or uncle                    |
| wife                  | vợ                    | the woman you are married to                   |
| husband               | chồng                 | the man you are married to                     |

> **Pattern**: Vietnamese distinguishes **older/younger sister** (chị vs em gái) and **older/younger brother** (anh vs em trai). English uses **one word** for both. To say age in English, add a word: "my **older** sister" / "my **younger** brother".

## Possessive pronouns — the "s" rule + apostrophe

When something belongs to a family member, you use a **possessive**.

> **Pattern**:
>
> - Singular noun + **`'s`**: `my brother's name` (the name of my brother)
> - Plural noun + **`s'`**: `my brothers' names` (the names of my brothers)

| Form                                | Example               |
| ----------------------------------- | --------------------- |
| My **mother's** name is Lan.        | (one mother)          |
| My **parents'** house is small.     | (mother + father)     |
| My **sister's** job is interesting. | (one sister)          |
| My **sisters'** jobs are different. | (two or more sisters) |

For pronouns, English uses different words:

| Subject pronoun | Possessive adjective | Possessive pronoun |
| --------------- | -------------------- | ------------------ |
| I               | **my**               | **mine**           |
| you             | your                 | yours              |
| he              | **his**              | his                |
| she             | **her**              | hers               |
| it              | its                  | its                |
| we              | our                  | ours               |
| they            | their                | theirs             |

> **Tip**: "This is **my** brother. **His** name is Tuan." "My" describes the noun that follows. "His" stands alone. The difference confuses many learners — practice both.

## Introducing your family — three patterns

### Pattern 1: "This is my..."

> **Pattern**: `This is my [family member]. [His/Her] name is [name].`

| English                               | Vietnamese                               |
| ------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------- |
| This is my mother. Her name is Lan.   | Đây là mẹ tôi. Tên bà là Lan.            |
| This is my father. His name is Hung.  | Đây là bố tôi. Tên ông là Hưng.          |
| This is my brother. His name is Tuan. | Đây là anh trai tôi. Tên anh ấy là Tuấn. |
| This is my sister. Her name is Mai.   | Đây là chị gái tôi. Tên chị ấy là Mai.   |

### Pattern 2: "I have..."

> **Pattern**: `I have [number] + [family member].`

| English              | Vietnamese               |
| -------------------- | ------------------------ |
| I have one brother.  | Tôi có một anh trai.     |
| I have two sisters.  | Tôi có hai chị em gái.   |
| I have no children.  | Tôi không có con.        |
| I have a big family. | Tôi có một gia đình lớn. |

### Pattern 3: "He / She is..."

> **Pattern**: `[Subject] is [age] [years old]. [Subject] is [adjective].`

| English                    | Vietnamese                 |
| -------------------------- | -------------------------- |
| My mother is 50 years old. | Mẹ tôi 50 tuổi.            |
| My father is very kind.    | Bố tôi rất tốt bụng.       |
| My brother is a teacher.   | Anh trai tôi là giáo viên. |
| My grandmother is patient. | Bà tôi kiên nhẫn.          |

## Listen to a real introduction

> **Anna**: Hi, Duc. Do you have siblings?
>
> **Duc**: Yes, I have one older brother and one younger sister.
>
> **Anna**: What does your brother do?
>
> **Duc**: He's a software engineer. He works in Ho Chi Minh City.
>
> **Anna**: And your sister?
>
> **Duc**: She's a student. She's studying medicine at university.
>
> **Anna**: Are your parents in Vietnam?
>
> **Duc**: Yes. My father is 60 and my mother is 58. They're both retired now.
>
> **Anna**: That's nice. Do you have a big extended family?
>
> **Duc**: Yes! I have two aunts, three uncles, and about ten cousins. Family gatherings are very busy.
>
> **Anna**: I only have one cousin. My family is small.

Notice how Duc uses **possessive adjectives** (my, your, his) and **he / she + is + age**. The conversation flows because Duc gives a new piece of information in every sentence.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ "I have **two brother**." — plural needs "s". ✅ "I have **two brothers**."
- ❌ "My mother name is Lan." — needs possessive. ✅ "My mother's name is Lan." or "**Her** name is Lan."
- ❌ "This is my sister, her name is Mai." — comma + comma-style is OK, but better with periods. ✅ "This is my sister. Her name is Mai."
- ❌ "I have 2 childs." — irregular plural. ✅ "I have 2 **children**."
- ❌ "My father is **age 60**." — wrong structure. ✅ "My father is **60 years old**."

## Culture note: family size and naming

**Vietnamese families** are often **multigenerational** — grandparents, parents, children, sometimes aunts and uncles all live together or very close. Family gatherings (lễ, Tết, giỗ) are huge events with 20-50 people.

**English-speaking families** are usually **nuclear** — parents and kids, often living far from grandparents. Saying "I have a big family" in English usually means 4-6 people. Saying "I have ten cousins" sounds unusual.

Also: Vietnamese uses **family terms as pronouns** (anh, chị, em, bác, cô) for non-family members of the right age. English uses **first names** for everyone except very formal contexts. Don't call your boss "anh" — just use "Mr. Smith" or "Sarah" if she invites you to.
