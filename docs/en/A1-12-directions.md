# Directions & Places

## Why directions matter

Getting lost is universal, and "Excuse me, where is the \_\_\_?" is the second most useful phrase in any travel situation (after "How much is this?"). Knowing ten direction words + five prepositions turns a confusing city into a navigable one.

## Direction words

| English         | Vietnamese | Icon |
| --------------- | ---------- | ---- |
| left            | trái       | ←    |
| right           | phải       | →    |
| straight        | thẳng      | ↑    |
| forward         | phía trước | ↑    |
| back / backward | phía sau   | ↓    |
| up              | lên trên   | ↑    |
| down            | xuống dưới | ↓    |
| north           | bắc        | ↑    |

> **Tip**: Compass directions (north, south, east, west) are useful on maps but rarely in casual conversation. "Turn left at the next corner" is more common than "Go east for two blocks."

## Prepositions of place

> **Pattern A — position**: `The bank is [preposition] the school.`
>
> **Pattern B — direction**: `Walk [direction] for [distance].`

## Asking for directions

> **Pattern A — Polite ask**: `Excuse me, where is the [place]?`
>
> **Pattern B — How to get somewhere**: `How do I get to [place]?`
>
> **Pattern C — Distance**: `Is it far from here?`

| English                       | Vietnamese                      | Tone                |
| ----------------------------- | ------------------------------- | ------------------- |
| Excuse me, where is the bank? | Xin lỗi, ngân hàng ở đâu?       | polite              |
| How do I get to the station?  | Làm sao để đến ga?              | needs route         |
| Is it far from here?          | Nó có xa từ đây không?          | asking distance     |
| Could you show me on the map? | Bạn chỉ trên bản đồ được không? | very polite         |
| I'm lost.                     | Tôi bị lạc.                     | admitting confusion |

> **Tip**: Always start with "Excuse me" when asking a stranger. Without it, you sound rude. Smiling helps.

## Giving directions

> **Pattern A — Single turn**: `Turn left / right.`
>
> **Pattern B — At a landmark**: `Turn left at the [traffic light / corner].`
>
> **Pattern C — Distance**: `Go straight for [two blocks / 100 meters].`
>
> **Pattern D — Final position**: `It's on your left / next to [place].`

### The complete recipe

| Step | Phrase                                              |
| ---- | --------------------------------------------------- |
| 1    | "Go straight for [distance]."                       |
| 2    | "Turn left at the [landmark]."                      |
| 3    | "Go straight for [distance]."                       |
| 4    | "Turn right at the [landmark]."                     |
| 5    | "It's [on your right / next to X / across from Y]." |

### Examples

| English                           | Vietnamese                 |
| --------------------------------- | -------------------------- |
| Go straight for two blocks.       | Đi thẳng hai dãy nhà.      |
| Turn left at the traffic light.   | Rẽ trái ở đèn giao thông.  |
| Turn right at the corner.         | Rẽ phải ở góc đường.       |
| Go past the bank.                 | Đi qua ngân hàng.          |
| It's on the corner of 5th Avenue. | Nó ở góc đường 5th Avenue. |
| It's next to the post office.     | Nó ở bên cạnh bưu điện.    |
| It's across from the park.        | Nó đối diện công viên.     |

> **"Go past X"** = walk beyond X, keep going. "Past" means "further than". "Walk past the bank" means "walk, going by the bank, keep going past it."

## Listen to a real direction conversation

> **Tourist**: Excuse me, could you help me? I'm looking for the post office.
>
> **Local**: Sure. The post office? It's not too far. Go straight for about 200 meters.
>
> **Tourist**: Go straight for 200 meters. OK.
>
> **Local**: Then turn left at the traffic light. You'll see a café on the corner.
>
> **Tourist**: Turn left at the traffic light. Got it.
>
> **Local**: After that, go straight for one more block. The post office is on your right, next to the bank.
>
> **Tourist**: On my right, next to the bank. Is it far from here?
>
> **Local**: About 5 minutes on foot. You can also take bus number 7. The stop is right there.
>
> **Tourist**: Thank you so much! You've been very helpful.
>
> **Local**: You're welcome. Have a great day!

Notice how the local breaks the route into 3-4 simple steps, names landmarks (traffic light, café, bank), and gives a backup option (bus).

## Common mistakes

- ❌ "Turn to left." — wrong preposition. ✅ "Turn **left**." (no "to")
- ❌ "Where is the bank **on**?" — wrong word. ✅ "Where is the bank?"
- ❌ "Go straight **two block**." — singular. ✅ "Go straight **two blocks**." (plural)
- ❌ "The bank is **in** the corner." — wrong. ✅ "The bank is **on** the corner."
- ❌ "The bank is **next the school**." — missing "to". ✅ "The bank is **next to** the school."

## Culture note: addresses and getting around

**Vietnam**: Street addresses often use **street name + ward + district** format. Many streets don't have numbers on buildings. "Tại số 15 Nguyễn Huệ, phường Bến Nghé, quận 1" — this is normal.

**US/UK**: Address format is **number + street name + city + zip**. "123 Main Street, Boston, MA 02101" — easy to find on Google Maps.

**Google Maps**: The fastest way to give someone directions in real time is to share your location via WhatsApp / Zalo / SMS and say "I'm sending you my location."

**"How do I get there?"**: In big US cities, people drive. In Vietnam, motorbikes are king. In London or Tokyo, the subway (underground / tube / metro) is fastest. Pick the right transport for the city.

## Scenario practice

### Situation

A visitor at the station needs to find a nearby hotel. Give three short direction steps and confirm the final side of the street.

> **Training note:** This situation and its details are fictional. They are designed for language practice, not presented as a real event, policy, study, or legal interpretation.

### Controlled practice

Choose three expressions from **Directions & Places**. Label each one as an opener, key detail, clarification, or next step. Remove any expression that does not fit the relationship in the situation.

### Guided task

Write a four-to-six-turn exchange for this situation. Use at least three target expressions, respond to the other speaker's information, and end with a question, confirmation, or agreed action.

### Model response

> **Partner:** We need to respond to the situation above. What would you say first?
>
> **You:** Excuse me, where is the bank?
>
> **Partner:** What detail should we confirm before we act?
>
> **You:** Turn left at the traffic light. Go straight for two blocks.

### Why the model works

The model shows one defensible route through the task; it is not a script to memorize. Compare its task completion, language accuracy, audience fit, and clarity with your response, then revise once.
