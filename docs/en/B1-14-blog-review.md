# Writing a Blog Post or Review

## What is a Review?

A review is a piece of writing where you share your opinion about a product, service, place, or experience.

> **Pattern**: `[Describe the experience]. Overall, I would [recommend/not recommend].`

## Structure of a Review

1. **Introduction**: What you are reviewing
2. **Body**: Details about your experience
3. **Conclusion**: Your recommendation

## Useful Phrases

### Opening

- `I recently tried/visited/experienced...`
- `I wanted to share my thoughts about...`
- `After trying this product, I feel...`

### Describing experiences

- `The atmosphere was...`
- `The staff were...`
- `The quality was...`
- `I was impressed by...`
- `I was disappointed by...`

### Making recommendations

- `I would definitely recommend...`
- `I would not recommend...`
- `Overall, it was...`
- `If you're looking for..., this is a great choice.`

## Example: Restaurant Review

> **My New Favorite Restaurant in London**
>
> I recently visited The Italian Kitchen with my family, and I have to say it was an incredible experience!
>
> The restaurant has a warm and welcoming atmosphere. The décor is beautiful, with soft lighting and comfortable seating. The staff were friendly and attentive throughout our meal.
>
> We ordered the pasta dishes, and they were absolutely delicious. The carbonara was creamy and perfectly seasoned. The portions were generous, and the prices were reasonable for the quality.
>
> Overall, I would definitely recommend The Italian Kitchen to anyone looking for authentic Italian food in London. It's perfect for a special occasion or a casual dinner with friends.

## Learning outcome

By the end of this lesson, you can write and revise a response about writing a blog post or review for a named audience, with a clear purpose, relevant support, and next step.

## Scenario practice

### Situation

Write a balanced review of a fictional restaurant used for a team dinner. Include atmosphere, service, one limitation, and a recommendation.

> **Training note:** This situation and its details are fictional. They are designed for language practice, not presented as a real event, policy, study, or legal interpretation.

### Controlled practice

Build a four-part outline: audience, purpose, one concrete supporting detail, and requested next step. Check that every planned sentence serves one of those four parts.

### Guided task

Write 45–135 words for the stated audience. Make the purpose visible in the opening, support it with a concrete detail, and close with a proportionate next step.

### Model response

> **Draft:** I recently tried this new restaurant and I have to say it was amazing! The atmosphere was cozy and the staff were really friendly. Overall, I would definitely recommend this place to anyone.
>
> **Revision note:** I organize the response so the situation comes first, the relevant detail follows, and the final sentence makes the requested action clear. I would revise any sentence that is correct but difficult for the audience to process. Other well-supported responses are possible.

### Why the model works

The model shows one defensible route through the task; it is not a script to memorize. Compare its task completion, language accuracy, audience fit, and clarity with your response, then revise once.

### Transfer task

Rewrite the review for a quiet client lunch. Keep the target language, but change the details and register needed for the new context.
