# Policy Document Reading

## Understanding policy language

Policy documents use specific language to distinguish between requirements, recommendations, and permissions.

### Obligation levels

| Word/Phrase  | Meaning     | Example                                              |
| ------------ | ----------- | ---------------------------------------------------- |
| must / shall | Mandatory   | "All employees must comply."                         |
| shall not    | Prohibited  | "External sharing shall not occur without approval." |
| may          | Permitted   | "Employees may request an exception."                |
| should       | Recommended | "Management should review quarterly."                |

## Structure of a policy document

| Section      | Purpose                        |
| ------------ | ------------------------------ |
| Purpose      | Why the policy exists          |
| Scope        | Who it applies to              |
| Definitions  | Key terms explained            |
| Requirements | Mandatory actions              |
| Exceptions   | Permitted deviations           |
| Enforcement  | Consequences of non-compliance |

## Identifying key obligations

In every policy, identify:

1. **Who** is affected (scope)
2. **What** they must do (requirements)
3. **When** it applies (effective date)
4. **Why** exceptions may exist (business rationale)

## Exercise: Classify the language

Read this excerpt and identify obligation levels:

> _"All personnel must complete the annual compliance training by March 31. Employees may request a 30-day extension with manager approval. Managers should ensure their teams are aware of this requirement."_

**Answers:**

- "must complete" → Mandatory
- "may request" → Permitted
- "should ensure" → Recommended

## Learning outcome

By the end of this lesson, you can extract the main point and supporting detail from a text about policy document reading, then make one evidence-based interpretation.

## Scenario practice

### Situation

Read a fictional data policy with a rule, exception, approval route, and supersession clause. Determine what is explicit and what requires clarification.

> **Training note:** This situation and its details are fictional. They are designed for language practice, not presented as a real event, policy, study, or legal interpretation.

### Controlled practice

Before reading the explanation again, write one line for the text's main point, one line for its strongest supporting detail, and one phrase that signals tone or stance.

### Guided task

Write 65–200 words as structured reading notes. State the main point, cite or paraphrase one supporting detail, identify a register choice, and separate evidence from your inference.

### Model response

> **Main point and evidence:** All employees must comply with the updated data protection policy. Exceptions to this policy require written approval from the Chief Compliance Officer. This policy supersedes all previous guidelines dated prior to January 2025.
>
> **Interpretation:** I select only the evidence that changes the decision, qualify any projection that depends on an assumption, and make ownership visible. A different stakeholder could reasonably prefer another option, so the response identifies the condition that would justify revisiting the recommendation. Other well-supported responses are possible.

### Why the model works

The model shows one defensible route through the task; it is not a script to memorize. Compare its task completion, language accuracy, audience fit, and clarity with your response, then revise once.

### Transfer task

Apply the policy to an urgent exception request. Keep the target language, but change the details and register needed for the new context.
