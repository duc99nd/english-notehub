# Policy Document Reading

## Understanding policy language

Policy documents use specific language to distinguish between requirements, recommendations, and permissions.

### Obligation levels

| Word/Phrase  | Meaning     | Example                                              |
| ------------ | ----------- | ---------------------------------------------------- |
| must / shall | Mandatory   | "All employees must comply."                         |
| shall not    | Prohibited  | "External sharing shall not occur without approval." |
| may          | Permitted   | "Employees may request an exception."                |
| should       | Recommended | "Management should review quarterly."                |

## Structure of a policy document

| Section      | Purpose                        |
| ------------ | ------------------------------ |
| Purpose      | Why the policy exists          |
| Scope        | Who it applies to              |
| Definitions  | Key terms explained            |
| Requirements | Mandatory actions              |
| Exceptions   | Permitted deviations           |
| Enforcement  | Consequences of non-compliance |

## Identifying key obligations

In every policy, identify:

1. **Who** is affected (scope)
2. **What** they must do (requirements)
3. **When** it applies (effective date)
4. **Why** exceptions may exist (business rationale)

## Exercise: Classify the language

Read this excerpt and identify obligation levels:

> _"All personnel must complete the annual compliance training by March 31. Employees may request a 30-day extension with manager approval. Managers should ensure their teams are aware of this requirement."_

**Answers:**

- "must complete" → Mandatory
- "may request" → Permitted
- "should ensure" → Recommended
