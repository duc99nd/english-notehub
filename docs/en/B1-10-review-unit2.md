# Review Unit 2

## What We Learned

In Unit 2, you learned four important skills for the workplace:

1. **Work & Daily Routines** - Vocabulary for jobs, workplace, and work activities
2. **Modals of Obligation & Advice** - must, have to, should, ought to
3. **Lifestyle Article** - Reading and understanding articles
4. **Job Application Letter** - Writing professional job applications

## Quick Review

### Work Vocabulary

| Category   | Examples                                               |
| ---------- | ------------------------------------------------------ |
| Jobs       | manager, employee, colleague, freelancer               |
| Workplace  | office, meeting room, desk, break room                 |
| Activities | attend a meeting, meet a deadline, give a presentation |

### Modals Review

| Modal    | Use                 | Example                    |
| -------- | ------------------- | -------------------------- |
| must     | internal obligation | I must finish this today.  |
| have to  | external obligation | I have to wear a uniform.  |
| should   | advice              | You should take a break.   |
| ought to | advice (formal)     | You ought to see a doctor. |

### Negative Forms

| Form          | Meaning       |
| ------------- | ------------- |
| mustn't       | prohibited    |
| don't have to | not necessary |
| shouldn't     | not advisable |

### Job Application Structure

1. Your address + Date
2. Recipient's address
3. Salutation: Dear Mr./Ms. [Name]
4. Opening: State the position
5. Body: Skills and experience
6. Closing: Thank and express interest
7. Sign-off: Yours faithfully

## Practice Activities

### Exercise 1: Choose the correct modal

1. You (must / should) see a doctor — you look pale.
2. I (have to / should) be at the airport by 6 PM.
3. You (mustn't / shouldn't) share passwords with anyone.

### Exercise 2: Complete the job application phrase

1. I am writing to \_\_\_ for the Marketing Manager position.
2. I have five years of \_\_\_ in digital marketing.
3. Thank you for \_\_\_ my application.

## Learning outcome

By the end of this lesson, you can combine language from the unit to resolve a realistic task involving review unit 2 and justify one revision.

## Scenario practice

### Situation

A colleague is preparing for an internal role. Explain the work routine, clarify obligations, select useful well-being advice, and draft an application paragraph.

> **Training note:** This situation and its details are fictional. They are designed for language practice, not presented as a real event, policy, study, or legal interpretation.

### Controlled practice

Select language from at least three lessons in this unit. For each choice, note the job it performs: describe, connect, qualify, request, or conclude.

### Guided task

Write 45–135 words to complete the integrated task. Use material from at least three lessons, then annotate one revision that improved accuracy, tone, or clarity.

### Model response

> **Integrated response:** Let's review what we learned in Unit 2. You can now talk about your job and workplace. You understand must, have to, should, and ought to.
>
> **Revision note:** I organize the response so the situation comes first, the relevant detail follows, and the final sentence makes the requested action clear. I would revise any sentence that is correct but difficult for the audience to process. Other well-supported responses are possible.

### Why the model works

The model shows one defensible route through the task; it is not a script to memorize. Compare its task completion, language accuracy, audience fit, and clarity with your response, then revise once.

### Transfer task

Change the role from marketing to customer support. Keep the target language, but change the details and register needed for the new context.
