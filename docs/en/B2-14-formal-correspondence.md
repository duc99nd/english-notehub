# Formal Correspondence

## Writing professional letters and emails

Formal English follows strict conventions for openings, closings, and tone. Use these templates for any professional communication.

### Standard structure

| Part     | Example                              |
| -------- | ------------------------------------ |
| Opening  | Dear Sir or Madam, / Dear Mr. Smith, |
| Purpose  | I am writing to inquire about...     |
| Detail   | I would be grateful if you could...  |
| Closing  | I look forward to hearing from you.  |
| Sign-off | Yours sincerely, / Yours faithfully, |

### Formal openings

- Dear Sir or Madam, (when name unknown)
- Dear Mr. / Ms. [Last Name], (when name known)
- To Whom It May Concern, (general)

### Formal closings

- Yours sincerely, (when name known)
- Yours faithfully, (when name unknown)
- Kind regards, (slightly less formal)

## Useful phrases

| English                               | Vietnamese                       |
| ------------------------------------- | -------------------------------- |
| I am writing to inquire about...      | Tôi viết để hỏi về...            |
| I would be grateful if you could...   | Tôi sẽ biết ơn nếu bạn có thể... |
| I look forward to hearing from you.   | Tôi mong nhận được hồi âm.       |
| At your earliest convenience          | Khi nào thuận tiện               |
| Please do not hesitate to contact me. | Đừng ngần ngại liên hệ với tôi.  |

## Sample email

> Dear Ms. Johnson,
>
> I am writing to inquire about the position advertised on your website. I would be grateful if you could provide further information about the role.
>
> I look forward to hearing from you at your earliest convenience.
>
> Yours sincerely,
> [Your Name]

## Learning outcome

By the end of this lesson, you can write and revise a response about formal correspondence for a named audience, with a clear purpose, relevant support, and next step.

## Scenario practice

### Situation

Write to a fictional vendor about a delayed service response. State the reference, request specific information, and set a courteous follow-up date.

> **Training note:** This situation and its details are fictional. They are designed for language practice, not presented as a real event, policy, study, or legal interpretation.

### Controlled practice

Build a four-part outline: audience, purpose, one concrete supporting detail, and requested next step. Check that every planned sentence serves one of those four parts.

### Guided task

Write 55–170 words for the stated audience. Make the purpose visible in the opening, support it with a concrete detail, and close with a proportionate next step.

### Model response

> **Draft:** I am writing to inquire about the position advertised on your website. I would be grateful if you could provide further information. I look forward to hearing from you at your earliest convenience.
>
> **Revision note:** I make the relationship between evidence, constraint, and recommendation explicit. The response avoids a binary claim when the situation calls for a trade-off, and it gives the audience a practical checkpoint for challenging or confirming the recommendation. Other well-supported responses are possible.

### Why the model works

The model shows one defensible route through the task; it is not a script to memorize. Compare its task completion, language accuracy, audience fit, and clarity with your response, then revise once.

### Transfer task

Rewrite the message after the vendor provides a partial answer. Keep the target language, but change the details and register needed for the new context.
