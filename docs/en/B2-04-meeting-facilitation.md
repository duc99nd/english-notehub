# Meeting Facilitation

## The language of effective facilitation

A good facilitator keeps meetings on track, manages time, and ensures everyone is heard. These phrases will help you lead meetings professionally.

> **Pattern**: `Let's kick off with...` — open the meeting with a clear agenda

### Opening and closing meetings

|| Phrase | Function |
|| --- | --- |
|| kick off | start the meeting |
|| round up | summarize key points |
|| park that topic | defer to later |
|| draw to a close | end the meeting |

### Managing discussion flow

|| Phrase | Function |
|| --- | --- |
|| tiebreaker | decisive vote or opinion to resolve deadlock |
|| table that for now | postpone a discussion |
|| open the floor | invite comments or questions |
|| play devil's advocate | challenge the prevailing view |

## Practical examples

**Opening a meeting**

> "Let's kick off with a quick round-up of last week's action items before we move to today's agenda."

**Deferring a topic**

> "That's a great point, but I think we should park that topic for the next session — we're running short on time."

**Breaking a deadlock**

> "We've been going back and forth on this for ten minutes. I think we need a tiebreaker to move things forward."

**Closing the meeting**

> "Let me draw today's meeting to a close. Action items will be sent by Friday."

## When to use each phrase

- **kick off**: Use when starting the meeting with energy and purpose
- **round up**: Use after discussions to summarize before moving on
- **park**: Use when a topic needs more time or context than available
- **tiebreaker**: Use when a group cannot reach consensus and needs a decision
- **devil's advocate**: Use to test the strength of an argument

## Practice lab

### Notice the language

Read the situation, then listen to the model once for meaning and once for phrasing.

> **Colleague:** How would you handle **meeting facilitation** in this situation?
>
> **You:** Let's kick off the meeting with a quick round-up.

Notice what the response does before you copy its wording: it gives the listener a clear point, enough context, and a usable next step.

> **Pattern**: clear point + concrete detail or reason + appropriate next step
