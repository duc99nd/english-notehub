# Travel Blogs & Postcards

## Reading Skills

When you read travel blogs and postcards, focus on:

- Main ideas and key details
- New vocabulary in context
- Descriptive language for places

## Reading 1: Travel Blog Post

### My Amazing Week in Barcelona

Hi everyone! I'm writing this from a small café near the beach. After three days here, I can say that Barcelona is one of the most beautiful cities I've ever visited.

The architecture is absolutely stunning — I've been to the Sagrada Familia twice and I still can't believe how incredible it is. Yesterday, I walked along Las Ramblas and got completely lost in the narrow streets of the Gothic Quarter. I stumbled upon a tiny restaurant where I had the most delicious seafood paella.

The weather has been amazing — sunny and warm every day. I've spent hours on the beach reading and swimming. Tonight, I'm planning to watch the sunset from Park Güell.

If you're thinking about visiting Barcelona, I highly recommend staying at least a week. There's so much to see and do!

---

## Reading 2: Postcard

> **From: Tom**
> **To: Sarah**
>
> Dear Sarah,
>
> Just a quick note from Greece! The islands are even more beautiful than I imagined. The water is crystal clear and the sunsets are unforgettable. Met some great people at the hostel — we're planning to rent a boat tomorrow!
>
> Wish you were here!
> Love, Tom

## Learning outcome

By the end of this lesson, you can extract the main point and supporting detail from a text about travel blogs & postcards, then make one evidence-based interpretation.

## Scenario practice

### Situation

Read a travel post written during a work trip. Identify the writer’s location, main impression, and one detail that supports it.

> **Training note:** This situation and its details are fictional. They are designed for language practice, not presented as a real event, policy, study, or legal interpretation.

### Controlled practice

Before reading the explanation again, write one line for the text's main point, one line for its strongest supporting detail, and one phrase that signals tone or stance.

### Guided task

Write 45–135 words as structured reading notes. State the main point, cite or paraphrase one supporting detail, identify a register choice, and separate evidence from your inference.

### Model response

> **Main point and evidence:** Hi everyone! I'm writing this from a small café in Rome. The weather has been amazing and I've been exploring every day. I can't wait to share more photos!
>
> **Interpretation:** I organize the response so the situation comes first, the relevant detail follows, and the final sentence makes the requested action clear. I would revise any sentence that is correct but difficult for the audience to process. Other well-supported responses are possible.

### Why the model works

The model shows one defensible route through the task; it is not a script to memorize. Compare its task completion, language accuracy, audience fit, and clarity with your response, then revise once.

### Transfer task

Change the post into a practical recommendation for a colleague. Keep the target language, but change the details and register needed for the new context.
