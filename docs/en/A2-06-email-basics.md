# Email Basics

## Parts of an email

Every email has these important parts:

1. **Subject line** - Brief topic of your email
2. **Greeting** - How you start
3. **Body** - Your message
4. **Closing** - How you end
5. **Signature** - Your name

## Formal greetings

> **Pattern**: `Dear [Name],` or `Dear Sir or Madam,`

| English                 | When to use                  |
| ----------------------- | ---------------------------- |
| Dear Mr. Smith,         | When you know the name       |
| Dear Sir or Madam,      | When you don't know the name |
| To Whom It May Concern, | Very formal                  |

## Informal greetings

| English       | When to use     |
| ------------- | --------------- |
| Hi [Name],    | With colleagues |
| Hello [Name], | Semi-formal     |

## Useful phrases

| English                             | Vietnamese                    |
| ----------------------------------- | ----------------------------- |
| I am writing to...                  | Tôi viết để...                |
| Thank you for your email.           | Cảm ơn bạn đã gửi email.      |
| I look forward to hearing from you. | Tôi mong chờ tin từ bạn.      |
| Please find attached...             | Vui lòng xem file đính kèm... |
| Best regards,                       | Trân trọng,                   |

## Email closings

- **Formal**: `Yours sincerely,` `Best regards,`
- **Informal**: `Cheers,` `Best,` `Thanks,`

## Learning outcome

By the end of this lesson, you can complete a four-turn exchange about email basics, choosing expressions that fit the relationship and immediate goal.

## Scenario practice

### Situation

Write a short email asking an administrator whether a meeting room is available on Friday afternoon.

> **Training note:** This situation and its details are fictional. They are designed for language practice, not presented as a real event, policy, study, or legal interpretation.

### Controlled practice

Choose three expressions from **Email Basics**. Label each one as an opener, key detail, clarification, or next step. Remove any expression that does not fit the relationship in the situation.

### Guided task

Write a four-to-six-turn exchange for this situation. Use at least three target expressions, respond to the other speaker's information, and end with a question, confirmation, or agreed action.

### Model response

> **Partner:** We need to respond to the situation above. What would you say first?
>
> **You:** Dear Sir or Madam, I am writing to inquire about your services.
>
> **Partner:** What detail should we confirm before we act?
>
> **You:** Thank you for your email. I look forward to hearing from you.

### Why the model works

The model shows one defensible route through the task; it is not a script to memorize. Compare its task completion, language accuracy, audience fit, and clarity with your response, then revise once.

### Transfer task

Turn the request into a polite reply confirming the room. Keep the target language, but change the details and register needed for the new context.
