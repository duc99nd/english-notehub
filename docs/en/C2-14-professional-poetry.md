# Professional Poetry

## Poetry in Professional Contexts

Professional poetry differs fundamentally from literary poetry: it serves **specific occasions** with **defined audiences** and **clear purposes**. The poet's role is not self-expression but service to the moment.

### Occasions for Professional Poetry

| Occasion                 | Function          | Tone                       |
| ------------------------ | ----------------- | -------------------------- |
| Commemorative speeches   | Mark significance | Ceremonial, dignified      |
| Corporate communications | Humanize messages | Warm, accessible           |
| Acceptance speeches      | Express gratitude | Humble, gracious           |
| Tributes                 | Honor individuals | Respectful, personal       |
| Inaugural addresses      | Set vision        | Inspiring, forward-looking |

## Craft Elements for Professional Poetry

### Meter: Creating Memorability

Meter creates the rhythm that makes poetry memorable:

| Meter             | Pattern                            | Use                          |
| ----------------- | ---------------------------------- | ---------------------------- |
| Iambic pentameter | da-DUM da-DUM da-DUM da-DUM da-DUM | Formal speeches, sonnets     |
| Iambic tetrameter | da-DUM da-DUM da-DUM da-DUM        | Commemorative verse          |
| Trochaic          | DUM-da DUM-da                      | More emphatic, insistent     |
| Anapestic         | da-da-DUM da-da-DUM                | Lighter, more conversational |

> **Pattern**: `The words must fall like rain on parched ground` (iambic pentameter)

### Rhyme: Creating Closure

Rhyme provides satisfying closure:

| Scheme    | Pattern  | Effect               |
| --------- | -------- | -------------------- |
| Couplet   | AA BB CC | Decisive ending      |
| Alternate | ABAB     | Balanced, flowing    |
| Enclosed  | ABBA     | Classical, contained |

> **Pattern**: `Not in my time, but in the time to come` — the rhyme signals completion while looking forward.

### Metaphor: Creating Meaning

Professional poetry relies on **relevant metaphor** that connects to the audience's experience:

> **Pattern**: `As the river shapes the stone, so too do we shape our institutions` (for institutional commemorations)

> **Pattern**: `What we build today becomes the foundation for tomorrow's dreams` (for groundbreaking ceremonies)

## Structural Patterns

### The Commemorative Structure

1. **Acknowledge** — recognize the occasion and those present
2. **Invoke** — call upon shared values or history
3. **Celebrate** — honor the subject or occasion
4. **Commit** — look forward, issue a call

> **Pattern (Opening)**: `We gather not to dwell on what's been done, but to celebrate what's yet to be.`

> **Pattern (Closing)**: `So let us carry forward what they've sown, and make their legacy our own.`

### The Tribute Structure

1. **Name and relationship** — establish connection
2. **Characteristic** — illuminate with specific detail
3. **Impact** — show consequence of their actions
4. **Legacy** — place in larger context

> **Pattern**: `She stood where others turned away; she spoke when silence was expected; she led where others feared to tread.`

## Professional Constraints

### Brevity

Professional poetry must be **concise** — audiences have limited attention:

| Occasion                | Typical length |
| ----------------------- | -------------- |
| Toast                   | 4-8 lines      |
| Commemorative address   | 16-32 lines    |
| Corporate annual report | 4-8 lines      |
| Acceptance speech       | 8-16 lines     |

### Accessibility

Avoid:

- Obscure references
- Overly complex syntax
- Self-indulgent imagery
- Personal grievances

Embrace:

- Clear diction
- Relevant metaphor
- Earnest emotion
- Appropriate rhythm
