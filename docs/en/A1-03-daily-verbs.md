# Daily Verbs

## Why these verbs first

If you can describe one day in English, you can already hold a real conversation. Twenty verbs cover most of what people say about daily life: wake up, eat, work, sleep, read, drink. Master these and your first English paragraph is yours.

## One day, told in verbs

> **Pattern**: `Subject + verb + (time / place / object)`
>
> `I wake up` at six in the morning.
>
> `I eat` breakfast at seven. I usually eat bread and `drink` coffee.
>
> `I go` to work at eight. My office is not far from home.
>
> `I work` from nine to five. I sit at my desk and `use` the computer.
>
> `I come` home at six. I am tired but happy.
>
> `I cook` dinner or `order` delivery at seven.
>
> `I watch` TV or `read` books in the evening.
>
> `I sleep` at ten or eleven.

That's eight verbs in nine sentences. Notice: every verb here is in the **base form** because the subject is **I**. Add "s" only when the subject is he/she/it (you'll see that in A1-04).

## Subject + verb — the most important rule in English

> **Pattern**:
>
> - `I` / `you` / `we` / `they` + **base verb** (no change)
> - `he` / `she` / `it` + **verb + s** (the "s" rule)

| Subject | Verb form | Example                           |
| ------- | --------- | --------------------------------- |
| I       | base      | I eat rice.                       |
| you     | base      | You eat rice.                     |
| we      | base      | We eat rice.                      |
| they    | base      | They eat rice.                    |
| he      | base + s  | **He eats** rice.                 |
| she     | base + s  | **She eats** rice.                |
| it      | base + s  | **It eats** rice. (for an animal) |

> **Note for Vietnamese speakers**: Vietnamese doesn't add "s" to verbs based on subject ("tôi ăn" / "cô ấy ăn" are identical). English DOES add "s" for he/she/it. This is one of the most common beginner mistakes.

## Common time phrases

Pair these verbs with time and you have 80% of daily-life sentences.

| Time phrase               | Use with                             |
| ------------------------- | ------------------------------------ |
| in the morning            | wake up, eat breakfast, drink coffee |
| in the afternoon          | work, study, run, walk               |
| in the evening            | cook, eat dinner, watch TV, read     |
| at night                  | sleep, read                          |
| at 6 / at seven o'clock   | any verb (specific time)             |
| every day / every morning | habits (no specific time)            |
| from 9 to 5               | work, study (range)                  |
| after work / before bed   | cook, read, sleep (sequence)         |

> **Pattern**: `I [verb] [time phrase]`. "I **eat** breakfast **in the morning**." "I **work** **from nine to five**."

## Listen to a real morning conversation

> **Duc**: What time do you wake up, Anna?
>
> **Anna**: I wake up at six thirty. I don't like waking up early, but I have to.
>
> **Duc**: Do you drink coffee?
>
> **Anna**: Yes! I drink two cups every morning. I can't work without coffee.
>
> **Duc**: Me too. I wake up at seven and drink coffee at home. I come to the office at eight thirty.
>
> **Anna**: That's late. I come to the office at eight. We have different routines.
>
> **Duc**: Yeah. By the way, do you cook dinner?
>
> **Anna**: Sometimes. I cook on weekdays. On weekends, I order delivery.
>
> **Duc**: Same. I cook on weekdays and eat out on Saturday.

Notice the pattern: **What time do you [verb]?** — the question is asked with base verb, but the answer uses base verb after "I" (no "s").

## Common mistakes

- ❌ "I **wakes** up at six." — I is base-verb subject. ✅ "I wake up at six."
- ❌ "She **wake** up at six." — She needs "s". ✅ "She wakes up at six."
- ❌ "I **drinking** coffee." — don't add "-ing" without "am". ✅ "I drink coffee." / "I am drinking coffee."
- ❌ "I wake up at six **clock**." — "o'clock" is one word, only with "at [number]". ✅ "I wake up at six o'clock." / "I wake up at six."
- ❌ "I wake up at morning." — needs "the". ✅ "I wake up **in the** morning."

## Culture note: schedules around the world

A typical workday in Vietnam often starts at 8:30 or 9:00. In the US and many European countries, **9 to 5** (or **9 to 6**) is the standard. Saying "I work nine to five" is so common it's a phrase — meaning any standard office job.

Coffee culture is very different too. Vietnamese coffee is often very strong and sweet, and people drink it throughout the day. In English-speaking offices, **two or three cups** is normal; many people switch to water or tea in the afternoon.
