# First & Second Conditionals

## Two Types of Conditionals

We use conditionals to talk about:

- **First Conditional**: Real and likely situations
- **Second Conditional**: Imaginary and unlikely situations

> **Pattern**: `If + present simple, will + verb`

## First Conditional

Use for real situations that are likely to happen.

### Structure

| If clause (condition) | Main clause (result) |
| --------------------- | -------------------- |
| If + present simple   | will + verb          |

> "If it **rains** tomorrow, I **will stay** at home."
> "If you **heat** water to 100°C, it **will boil**."
> "If I **see** her, I **will tell** her."

### Common time expressions

- tomorrow, next week, soon, if...

## Second Conditional

Use for imaginary or unlikely situations.

### Structure

| If clause (condition) | Main clause (result) |
| --------------------- | -------------------- |
| If + past simple      | would + verb         |

> "If I **won** the lottery, I **would travel** the world."
> "If she **were** richer, she **would buy** a bigger house."
> "If I **had** more time, I **would learn** Spanish."

### Note on "were"

We often use **were** instead of "was" for all subjects in the Second Conditional:

> "If I **were** you, I **would accept** the offer."

## Quick Comparison

| Type               | Use                 | Example                                             |
| ------------------ | ------------------- | --------------------------------------------------- |
| First Conditional  | Real, likely        | If it **rains**, I **will stay** home.              |
| Second Conditional | Imaginary, unlikely | If I **had** a million dollars, I **would retire**. |

## When to Use Each

| First Conditional  | Second Conditional       |
| ------------------ | ------------------------ |
| Tomorrow's weather | Winning the lottery      |
| Future plans       | Being in a different job |
| Likely events      | Dreams and wishes        |

## Examples in Context

> **First Conditional (planning):**
> "If the flight **is** delayed, we **will miss** the connection."
> "If you **don't finish** your homework, you **will get** in trouble."

> **Second Conditional (imagining):**
> "If I **were** a famous chef, I **would open** a restaurant."
> "If you **could** speak any language, which **would you choose**?"

## Learning outcome

By the end of this lesson, you can produce a connected response about first & second conditionals, use the target structure accurately, and explain one effect of that choice.

## Scenario practice

### Situation

Plan an outdoor team event with uncertain weather. State a likely contingency and an imaginary alternative with a larger budget.

> **Training note:** This situation and its details are fictional. They are designed for language practice, not presented as a real event, policy, study, or legal interpretation.

### Controlled practice

Take two example sentences from the lesson. Transform the first with the target structure, then revise the second to change emphasis or level of certainty without changing the core facts.

### Guided task

Write 45–135 words for the situation. Use the target structure at least twice, underline those choices, and add one sentence explaining how one structure changes emphasis, time, condition, or certainty.

### Model response

> **Draft:** If it rains tomorrow, I'll stay at home. If I won the lottery, I would travel the world. If I had more time, I would learn to cook.
>
> **Language choice:** I organize the response so the situation comes first, the relevant detail follows, and the final sentence makes the requested action clear. I would revise any sentence that is correct but difficult for the audience to process. Other well-supported responses are possible.

### Why the model works

The model shows one defensible route through the task; it is not a script to memorize. Compare its task completion, language accuracy, audience fit, and clarity with your response, then revise once.

### Transfer task

Change the risk from rain to low attendance. Keep the target language, but change the details and register needed for the new context.
