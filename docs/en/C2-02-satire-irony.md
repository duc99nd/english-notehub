# Satire & Irony

## Defining the Terms

### Satire

Satire uses **humor, irony, and exaggeration** to critique flaws in individuals, institutions, or society. It aims to expose and shame.

> **Pattern**: `The politician's promise to cut taxes was met with deserved derision.`

### Verbal Irony

Verbal irony occurs when someone **says the opposite** of what they mean. The intent is usually to mock or emphasize a point.

> **Pattern**: `Oh, brilliant—you've just proven you can't read instructions.`

### Sarcasm

Sarcasm is a **harsher form** of verbal irony, often with a bitter or cutting tone. It frequently employs the subjunctive mood for effect.

> **Pattern**: `If only you had thought to read the manual beforehand.` (subjunctive)

## Grammatical Markers of Irony

| Marker               | Example                                           | Effect                    |
| -------------------- | ------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------- |
| Subjunctive mood     | "If only you were here" (when glad someone isn't) | Contrasts reality         |
| Hyperbole            | "I've told you a million times"                   | Exaggeration for emphasis |
| Litotes              | "Not bad" (when something is terrible)            | Understatement            |
| Rhetorical questions | "Who would have thought?"                         | Challenges expectation    |

## Types of Irony

### Situational Irony

When **outcomes are opposite** to what is expected:

> **Pattern**: A fire station burns down.

### Dramatic Irony

When the **audience knows more** than the characters:

> **Pattern**: In a horror film, viewers see the killer in the house while the character says, "We're safe here."

## Satire in Political Discourse

Political satire often combines multiple irony types:

> **Pattern**: `"The senator's commitment to transparency was evident in the 847 pages of redacted documents."`

This sentence uses:

- Understatement ("commitment to transparency")
- Hyperbole (specific number for emphasis)
- Implicit critique (redaction contradicts transparency)

## Analyzing Satire

When encountering satire, ask:

1. What is being criticized?
2. What irony techniques are used?
3. What is the intended audience's reaction?
