# Executive Summary Writing

## The BLUF principle

Effective executive summaries follow **BLUF** — **B**ottom **L**ine **U**p **F**ront.

> **Pattern**: `Recommendation + key reason + supporting facts`

| Component | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| Recommendation | What you want the reader to do |
| Key reason | Why this matters now |
| Supporting facts | 2-3 pieces of evidence |
| Risks | What could go wrong |
| Ask | What you need |

## Template

```
[Bold recommendation sentence — one line]

[1-2 sentences of context: what is being proposed and why now]

[2-3 bullets of supporting data]

[1 sentence on key risk and mitigation]

Ask: [Specific decision/action needed by specific date].
```

## Sample executive summary

> **Management recommends approval of a $5M investment in operational efficiency, projected to deliver 15% cost reduction over 18 months.**
>
> The proposed investment funds automation of three core processes, addressing a documented 22% productivity gap against industry benchmarks. Pilot results showed 12% cost reduction in Q3 alone, and full rollout is expected to recover costs within 14 months.
>
> Key risk: employee transition. Mitigation: comprehensive retraining program with $400K budgeted.
>
> **Ask:** Board approval at the November 14 meeting.

## Avoid these mistakes

| Mistake | Better |
| --- | --- |
| Leading with background | Lead with recommendation |
| Burying the ask | State the ask explicitly |
| Too many details | Use 2-3 key data points |
| Vague numbers | Be specific ($5M, 18 months) |
