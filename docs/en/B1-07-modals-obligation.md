# Modals of Obligation & Advice

## Types of Modals

We use modals to express:

- **Obligation** (bắt buộc)
- **Advice** (lời khuyên)
- **Necessity** (sự cần thiết)

> **Pattern**: `Subject + modal + verb`

## Must vs Have to

| Modal       | Use                               | Example                       |
| ----------- | --------------------------------- | ----------------------------- |
| **must**    | Internal obligation (you decide)  | I must finish this today.     |
| **have to** | External obligation (rules, laws) | I have to be at work at 9 AM. |

> "You **must** pass the exam to graduate." (necessary requirement)
> "I **have to** wear a uniform at work." (company rule)

## Should and Ought to

Use for advice and recommendations.

| Modal        | Example                        |
| ------------ | ------------------------------ |
| **should**   | You **should** take a break.   |
| **ought to** | You **ought to** see a doctor. |

> "You **should** review your notes before the test."
> "You **ought to** apologize for being late."

## Negative Forms

| Modal                  | Negative      | Meaning                   |
| ---------------------- | ------------- | ------------------------- |
| must not (mustn't)     | mustn't       | prohibited (không được)   |
| don't have to          | don't have to | not necessary (không cần) |
| should not (shouldn't) | shouldn't     | not advisable (không nên) |

> "You **mustn't** smoke in the office." (prohibited)
> "You **don't have to** come early tomorrow." (optional)
> "You **shouldn't** worry about small things." (advice not to)

## Past Forms

| Present | Past                          |
| ------- | ----------------------------- |
| must    | had to                        |
| should  | should have (for past advice) |

> "I **had to** work late yesterday."
> "You **should have** told me earlier."

## Examples in Context

> **At work:**
> "You **must** submit the report by Friday."
> "You **have to** attend the training session."
> "You **should** ask your manager for help."

> **Giving advice:**
> "You **should** take more breaks."
> "You **ought to** learn a new skill."
> "You **shouldn't** skip lunch."

## Scenario practice

### Situation

Explain an onboarding checklist: distinguish a firm requirement, practical necessity, recommendation, and past obligation.

> **Training note:** This situation and its details are fictional. They are designed for language practice, not presented as a real event, policy, study, or legal interpretation.

### Controlled practice

Take two example sentences from the lesson. Transform the first with the target structure, then revise the second to change emphasis or level of certainty without changing the core facts.

### Guided task

Write 45–135 words for the situation. Use the target structure at least twice, underline those choices, and add one sentence explaining how one structure changes emphasis, time, condition, or certainty.

### Model response

> **Draft:** You must submit the report by Friday. You should take a break — you've been working all day. I had to reschedule the meeting because of the holiday.
>
> **Language choice:** I organize the response so the situation comes first, the relevant detail follows, and the final sentence makes the requested action clear. I would revise any sentence that is correct but difficult for the audience to process. Other well-supported responses are possible.

### Why the model works

The model shows one defensible route through the task; it is not a script to memorize. Compare its task completion, language accuracy, audience fit, and clarity with your response, then revise once.

### Transfer task

Soften the advice for an experienced contractor. Keep the target language, but change the details and register needed for the new context.
