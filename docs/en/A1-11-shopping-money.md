# Shopping & Money

## Why shopping words matter

In any English-speaking country, you'll shop within 24 hours of arrival: a coffee, a SIM card, a t-shirt, a metro ticket. Knowing how to ask "How much is this?" and understand the answer saves you money and stress. Twenty words cover 90% of shopping situations.

## Two big questions: How much vs. How many

> **Pattern A — How much + uncountable (price, quantity of liquids)**: `How much is this?` `How much water do you drink?`
>
> **Pattern B — How many + countable (number)**: `How many apples do you want?` `How many shirts are there?`

| Question               | Use for             | Example                            |
| ---------------------- | ------------------- | ---------------------------------- |
| How much is this?      | price of one item   | "How much is this shirt?"          |
| How much are these?    | price of many items | "How much are these apples?"       |
| How much does it cost? | price (formal)      | "How much does this phone cost?"   |
| How many do you want?  | quantity            | "How many coffees would you like?" |
| How many are there?    | number              | "How many shirts do you have?"     |

## Numbers and prices

> **Pattern**: `It costs [number] [currency].`

| English                             | Vietnamese          |
| ----------------------------------- | ------------------- |
| It costs $10.                       | Nó giá 10 đô.       |
| It costs 50,000 dong.               | Nó giá 50.000 đồng. |
| It costs £5.50 (five pounds fifty). | Nó giá 5,50 bảng.   |
| It costs €20 (twenty euros).        | Nó giá 20 euro.     |

> **Tip**: When saying decimals, say "**point**" or "**and**" between whole and part. "$5.50" = "five dollars **fifty**" or "five dollars **and fifty cents**".

## Shopping phrases — the script

> **Pattern A — Ask price**: `How much is this?` / `How much does this cost?`
>
> **Pattern B — Express opinion**: `It's too expensive.`
>
> **Pattern C — Negotiate**: `Can you give me a discount?`
>
> **Pattern D — Pay**: `Can I pay by card?` / `I'll pay in cash.`

| Step                    | What to say                                         |
| ----------------------- | --------------------------------------------------- |
| 1. Browse               | (Look around. Pick up an item.)                     |
| 2. Ask price            | "Excuse me, how much is this?"                      |
| 3. Negotiate (optional) | "It's a bit expensive. Can you give me a discount?" |
| 4. Decide               | "I'll take it." / "Just looking, thanks."           |
| 5. Pay                  | "Can I pay by card?" / "I'll pay in cash."          |
| 6. Get change           | "Keep the change, thanks."                          |
| 7. Receipt              | "Can I have a receipt, please?"                     |

> **Tip**: "I'll take it" means "I'll buy it". "Just looking, thanks" means "I don't need help right now" — use this when a shop assistant approaches you.

## Listen to a real shopping conversation

> **Shop assistant**: Hi, can I help you?
>
> **Anna**: Yes, how much is this jacket?
>
> **Shop assistant**: It's 800,000 dong.
>
> **Anna**: Hmm, that's a bit expensive. Do you have a discount?
>
> **Shop assistant**: Today only, we have a 20% sale. So it's 640,000.
>
> **Anna**: Still kind of expensive. Can you give me a better price?
>
> **Shop assistant**: I can give you 600,000. That's the best I can do.
>
> **Anna**: OK, I'll take it. Can I pay by card?
>
> **Shop assistant**: Sure. Cash or credit?
>
> **Anna**: Credit. Do you accept Visa?
>
> **Shop assistant**: Yes, we take Visa and Mastercard.
>
> **Anna**: Great. Can I have a receipt, please?
>
> **Shop assistant**: Of course. Here you go. Thank you!
>
> **Anna**: Thank you. Have a nice day.

Notice how Anna negotiates politely ("a bit expensive", "a better price"). In Vietnam, haggling is normal. In US department stores, prices are fixed; in markets everywhere, you can ask for a better price.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ "How **many** is this shirt?" — shirts are about price. ✅ "How **much** is this shirt?"
- ❌ "It **cost** 50 dollars." — for present habits. ✅ "It **costs** 50 dollars." (present) / "It **cost** 50 dollars." (past)
- ❌ "I pay **by** cash." — both work but "in" is more common. ✅ "I pay **in** cash." / "I pay **by** card."
- ❌ "The shirt is 10 dollars **cheap**." — cheap is an adjective, not a quantity. ✅ "The shirt is cheap." / "It's only 10 dollars."
- ❌ "Can I have a **recipe**?" — wrong word. ✅ "Can I have a **receipt**?" (receipt = hóa đơn; recipe = công thức nấu ăn)

## Culture note: prices, tipping, and haggling

**Haggling**: In Vietnam, prices at markets are usually negotiable. In supermarkets and shopping malls, prices are fixed. In US/UK supermarkets, prices are always fixed.

**Tipping**: In the US, restaurants expect 15-20% tip. Tipping in shops is not expected. In Vietnam, no tipping culture for shopping.

**Currency**: "Dollar" in English usually means US dollar ($). For Vietnamese currency, say "dong" or "đồng" (or write 50,000 VND). For British money, say "pounds" (£). For European money, say "euros" (€).

**Sizes**: US/UK clothing sizes run small (US 4 = Vietnamese "S"). Shoe sizes are different too. In Vietnam, ask for size by S/M/L or by number.
