# Complex Sentence Structures

## Building sophisticated sentences

Complex sentences use subordinate clauses to add nuance and detail. Mastering these helps you sound polished and professional.

### Common subordinating conjunctions

| Conjunction                | Function   | Example                                          |
| -------------------------- | ---------- | ------------------------------------------------ |
| although / even though     | Contrast   | Although the deadline is tight, we will deliver. |
| because / since / as       | Cause      | Because of the delay, we need more time.         |
| while / whereas            | Comparison | She prefers remote, while he prefers the office. |
| if / unless                | Condition  | Unless we act now, we risk losing ground.        |
| when / once / as soon as   | Time       | Once approved, we will start.                    |
| given that / provided that | Condition  | Given the complexity, we need more resources.    |

## Inversion for emphasis

> **Pattern**: `Negative adverbial + auxiliary + subject + verb`

| English                                                          | Vietnamese                                                 |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- |
| Not only does it reduce costs, but it also improves scalability. | Không chỉ giảm chi phí, mà còn cải thiện khả năng mở rộng. |
| Hardly had I arrived when the meeting started.                   | Tôi vừa đến nơi thì cuộc họp bắt đầu.                      |

## Participle clauses

> **Pattern**: `V-ing/V-ed + [rest of sentence]`

- Given the complexity of the project, we recommend additional resources.
- Having reviewed the data, we agree with the proposal.
- Considered from all angles, the decision seems sound.

## Practice lab

### Notice the language

Read the situation, then listen to the model once for meaning and once for phrasing.

> **Colleague:** How would you handle **complex sentence structures** in this situation?
>
> **You:** Although the deadline is tight, we will deliver quality work.

Notice what the response does before you copy its wording: it gives the listener a clear point, enough context, and a usable next step.

> **Pattern**: clear point + concrete detail or reason + appropriate next step

### Your turn

Write four connected sentences for a colleague or classmate. Use the target structure twice, then revise one sentence to change its tone or emphasis.

### Model response

> Although the deadline is tight, we will deliver quality work. Given the complexity of the project, we recommend additional resources. Unless we act now, we risk losing our competitive edge.

The model is one possible response, not a script you must memorize. Compare its purpose, level of detail, and tone with your version, then revise once.

### Register switch

Rewrite your response twice: first for a trusted teammate, then for a client or senior stakeholder. Keep the underlying message the same while changing directness, context, and the amount of explanation. Identify one phrase you softened, one detail you made explicit, and one assumption you removed.
