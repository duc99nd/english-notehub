# Review Unit 1

## What We Learned

In Unit 1, you learned four important skills for talking about travel:

1. **Travel & Transport Vocabulary** - Words for transport, travel activities, and useful phrases
2. **Past Tenses & Narratives** - Past Simple, Past Continuous, and Past Perfect
3. **Travel Blogs & Reading** - Reading real travel writing
4. **Email to a Friend** - Writing personal, friendly emails

## Quick Review

### Travel Vocabulary

| Category   | Examples                                 |
| ---------- | ---------------------------------------- |
| Transport  | flight, train, bus, taxi, ferry          |
| Activities | to book, check in, board, depart, arrive |
| At airport | boarding pass, luggage, check-in desk    |

### Past Tenses

| Tense           | Use                                | Example                             |
| --------------- | ---------------------------------- | ----------------------------------- |
| Past Simple     | Completed actions                  | I **went** to Paris.                |
| Past Continuous | Actions in progress                | I **was sleeping** when you called. |
| Past Perfect    | Actions before another past action | I **had left** before she arrived.  |

### Time Connectors

- when, while, before, after, by the time, as soon as

### Email Structure

1. Greeting: Hi [Name]
2. Opening: How are you? It's been a while...
3. Body: Main message
4. Closing: Love / Best / Take care
5. Signature: Your name

## Practice Activities

### Exercise 1: Choose the correct tense

1. I (was reading / read) when the phone rang.
2. She (has left / had left) before I got home.
3. They (went / was going) to the beach yesterday.

### Exercise 2: Write the travel vocabulary

1. A ticket for a plane journey: \_\_\_
2. To get on a plane: \_\_\_
3. A gift from a trip: \_\_\_

## Learning outcome

By the end of this lesson, you can combine language from the unit to resolve a realistic task involving review unit 1 and justify one revision.

## Scenario practice

### Situation

A delayed journey affects a client visit. Explain the route, narrate what happened, summarize a useful travel post, and email an update.

> **Training note:** This situation and its details are fictional. They are designed for language practice, not presented as a real event, policy, study, or legal interpretation.

### Controlled practice

Select language from at least three lessons in this unit. For each choice, note the job it performs: describe, connect, qualify, request, or conclude.

### Guided task

Write 45–135 words to complete the integrated task. Use material from at least three lessons, then annotate one revision that improved accuracy, tone, or clarity.

### Model response

> **Integrated response:** Let's review what we learned in Unit 1. You can now talk about travel and transport. You can describe past events using different tenses.
>
> **Revision note:** I organize the response so the situation comes first, the relevant detail follows, and the final sentence makes the requested action clear. I would revise any sentence that is correct but difficult for the audience to process. Other well-supported responses are possible.

### Why the model works

The model shows one defensible route through the task; it is not a script to memorize. Compare its task completion, language accuracy, audience fit, and clarity with your response, then revise once.

### Transfer task

Change the audience from a friend to a project manager. Keep the target language, but change the details and register needed for the new context.
