# Review Unit 2

## Unit 2 Summary

Unit 2 has expanded your C2 repertoire into the realm of professional and institutional discourse: conference presentations, formal panels, diplomatic correspondence, and legal documents.

## Key Concepts Recap

### 1. Conference Talk Vocabulary (C2-06)

Conference discourse follows established rhetorical conventions. Your mastery of opening formulas, transitional phrases, and audience engagement vocabulary enables both delivery and professional participation.

| Category   | Key Phrases                                                       |
| ---------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Opening    | "I shall argue that...", "Today's talk is divided into..."        |
| Transition | "Turning now to...", "Building on this..."                        |
| Q&A        | "I'm glad you raised that point...", "That's a fair challenge..." |
| Closing    | "To conclude...", "I look forward to discussing..."               |

### 2. Panel Discussion Grammar (C2-07)

Panel discourse demands sophisticated grammatical range:

- **Second conditional** softens disagreement
- **Subjunctive mood** appears in formal proposals
- **Participial constructions** create elegant concision
- **Inversion** signals formal register

**Key structures:**

- "Were the committee to approve..." (inverted conditional)
- "It is essential that he be present" (subjunctive)
- "Having considered the evidence, the panel recommends..."

### 3. Diplomatic Language (C2-08)

Diplomatic register prioritizes ambiguity management and face-saving. Understanding what is NOT said is as important as what IS said.

**Escalation spectrum:**

```
"Expresses concern" → "Expresses grave concern" → "Expresses deep concern" → "Reserves the right to..."
```

(Mild ←———————————————————————————————————————————→ Ominous)

### 4. Legal Contracts (C2-09)

Legal writing demands precision over elegance. Modal verb choices create binding obligations.

**Obligation hierarchy:**

```
"Shall" (mandatory) > "Must" (mandatory) > "May" (permissive) > "Should" (hortatory)
```

(Strongest obligation ←——————————————————————————————————→ Weakest)

## Cross-Cutting Themes

Notice how these skills interweave:

- **Conference discourse uses hedging** — polite disagreement mirrors diplomatic language
- **Panel grammar requires formality** — subjunctive and inversion signal elevated register
- **Diplomatic language manages ambiguity** — similar precision goals to legal drafting
- **Legal writing demands absolute clarity** — contrasts with diplomatic intentional vagueness
