# Formal Debate Grammar

## Grammatical Tools for Persuasion

Formal debate deploys specific grammatical structures to maximize **rhetorical impact**, create **emphasis**, and maintain **logical coherence**. Mastery of these patterns distinguishes elite debaters.

### Cleft Sentences for Emphasis

Cleft sentences isolate and emphasize key elements:

> **Pattern**: `It is [not X] but [Y] that matters.`

> **Pattern**: `What the opposition fails to acknowledge is [X].`

> **Pattern**: `The real issue is not [X], but rather [Y].`

#### Application in Debate

> **Pattern**: `It is not that we oppose reform; it is that we oppose this reform.`

> **Pattern**: `What the government has failed to grasp is the systemic nature of the problem.`

### Passive Constructions for Impersonal Attribution

Passives allow you to attribute claims without naming agents:

> **Pattern**: `It has been demonstrated that [claim].` (who demonstrated is irrelevant)

> **Pattern**: `Evidence suggests that...` (hedging with passive)

> **Pattern**: `This position has been challenged by recent scholarship.` (recent scholarship as agent)

#### Strategic Passive Usage

| Function  | Example                      | Effect                     |
| --------- | ---------------------------- | -------------------------- |
| De-agent  | "Mistakes were made"         | Avoids blame               |
| Authority | "Research has shown..."      | Impersonal credibility     |
| Hedging   | "It might be argued..."      | Softens claim              |
| Focus     | "The policy was implemented" | Shifts attention to object |

### Emphatic "Do" for Assertion

The emphatic "do" adds force to assertions:

> **Pattern**: `The data do show a correlation.`

> **Pattern**: `I do maintain that the methodology is flawed.`

### Inversions for Formal Register

Inversion signals elevated formality:

> **Pattern**: `Never has a policy had such far-reaching implications.`

> **Pattern**: `Rarely do we encounter such a clear-cut case.`

> **Pattern**: `Little did the opposition anticipate this line of argumentation.`

## Debate-Specific Structures

### Rebuttal Grammar

> **Pattern**: `While the opposition correctly identifies [X], they fail to account for [Y].`

> **Pattern**: `The argument rests on a false dichotomy between [X] and [Y].`

> **Pattern**: `This is not merely [X]; it is fundamentally [Y]—and the distinction matters.`

### Point of Information Responses

When accepting or declining points of information:

> **Pattern**: `I am grateful for the intervention; however, I have not yet finished my point.`

> **Pattern**: `I gladly accept this point, which indeed supports my argument that...`

### Weighing and Prioritizing

> **Pattern**: `The opposition's arguments, while relevant, are outweighed by considerations of [X].`

> **Pattern**: `On balance, the evidence tips decisively in favour of [position].`

## Logical Connectors in Debate

| Function    | Connector          | Example                               |
| ----------- | ------------------ | ------------------------------------- |
| Concession  | "Admittedly..."    | "Admittedly, X, but Y"                |
| Rebuttal    | "However..."       | "However, the evidence shows..."      |
| Emphasis    | "Indeed..."        | "Indeed, the very opposite is true"   |
| Alternative | "Alternatively..." | "Alternatively, we might consider..." |
