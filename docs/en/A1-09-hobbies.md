# Hobbies & Free Time

## Why hobbies matter early

"How do you spend your free time?" is the third most common question in English small-talk (after "how are you" and "what do you do"). Knowing ten hobbies + three verbs of preference lets you answer this question in five different ways.

> **Verb choice matters**: "play" goes with **instruments and team sports**. "go + to + the + noun" goes with **places** (go to the gym, go to the café). "do" is the catch-all for activities (do yoga, do exercise).

## The "play" rule

> **Pattern**: `play [instrument]` and `play [sport with ball]`

| English                           | Why "play"             |
| --------------------------------- | ---------------------- |
| play the piano / guitar / drums   | instrument             |
| play soccer / basketball / tennis | team sport with a ball |
| play games / video games          | competitive activity   |

> **Common mistake**: ❌ "I **listen** music." — "listen" needs "**to**". ✅ "I listen **to** music." ❌ "I **play** music." — possible but means "perform music". The standard phrase is "I **listen to** music."

## Three verbs of preference: like, love, enjoy

| Verb  | Strength | Pattern                                   | Example          |
| ----- | -------- | ----------------------------------------- | ---------------- |
| like  | neutral  | `I like to [verb]` or `I like [verb-ing]` | I like to read.  |
| love  | strong   | `I love to [verb]` or `I love [verb-ing]` | I love cooking.  |
| enjoy | formal   | `I enjoy [verb-ing]` (no "to")            | I enjoy reading. |

> **Pattern**: With **enjoy**, the verb must be **-ing**, NOT "to + verb". This is the opposite of "like" and "love".
>
> - ✅ "I like **to read**." / "I like **reading**." (both work)
> - ✅ "I love **to cook**." / "I love **cooking**." (both work)
> - ✅ "I enjoy **reading**." (NOT ❌ "I enjoy **to read**.")

## Frequency words — how often?

| Word                 | Frequency | Example                          |
| -------------------- | --------- | -------------------------------- |
| always               | 100%      | I always drink coffee.           |
| usually              | ~80%      | I usually read at night.         |
| often                | ~60-70%   | I often play soccer on weekends. |
| sometimes            | ~30-40%   | I sometimes watch movies.        |
| rarely / hardly ever | ~10%      | I rarely play games.             |
| never                | 0%        | I never play tennis.             |

> **Pattern**: `frequency word + subject + verb`. "I **often** read." "She **sometimes** cooks."

## Listen to a real hobby conversation

> **Anna**: What do you like to do in your free time, Duc?
>
> **Duc**: I love reading. I read every night before I sleep.
>
> **Anna**: That's nice. I love reading too. What kind of books?
>
> **Duc**: Mostly business books. I enjoy learning about startups and technology.
>
> **Anna**: Cool. Do you play any sports?
>
> **Duc**: Sometimes. I play soccer with my friends on Saturday. I also go to the gym three times a week.
>
> **Anna**: That's impressive. I'm not very active. I enjoy listening to music and watching movies. I also love cooking.
>
> **Duc**: Oh, what do you cook?
>
> **Anna**: Mostly Vietnamese food. Pho, spring rolls, banh mi. I love trying new recipes.
>
> **Duc**: Wow, I'd love to try. I can't cook at all. I always order delivery.
>
> **Anna**: I can teach you! Cooking is easier than you think.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ "I **enjoy to read** books." — enjoy + -ing only. ✅ "I enjoy **reading** books."
- ❌ "I **listen music** every day." — listen needs "to". ✅ "I listen **to** music."
- ❌ "I **make** photos." — wrong verb. ✅ "I **take** photos."
- ❌ "I **do** soccer." — wrong verb. ✅ "I **play** soccer."
- ❌ "I like **listen** music." — two errors. ✅ "I like **listening to** music." or "I like **to listen to** music."

## Culture note: hobbies across cultures

**Vietnam**: Common hobbies include cooking, watching films, reading, social media, café hopping, karaoke, traveling. Sports like badminton and football are popular. Many young people enjoy gaming (Liên Quân, Free Fire) and livestreaming.

**US / UK**: Gym culture is much stronger. Running, cycling, hiking are common. "Binge-watching" TV shows on Netflix is a standard hobby. Yard work (gardening, lawn care) is a hobby in the US.

**A useful phrase**: "What do you do for fun?" — same as "What are your hobbies?" but more casual.

**Saying you don't have hobbies**: "I'm kind of boring" or "I don't have many hobbies" is normal in English. You don't need to defend it.

## Learning outcome

By the end of this lesson, you can write and revise a response about hobbies & free time for a named audience, with a clear purpose, relevant support, and next step.

## Scenario practice

### Situation

A new friend asks what you do after work or class. Write four short sentences about two hobbies and when you do them.

> **Training note:** This situation and its details are fictional. They are designed for language practice, not presented as a real event, policy, study, or legal interpretation.

### Controlled practice

Build a four-part outline: audience, purpose, one concrete supporting detail, and requested next step. Check that every planned sentence serves one of those four parts.

### Guided task

Write 20–70 words for the stated audience. Make the purpose visible in the opening, support it with a concrete detail, and close with a proportionate next step.

### Model response

> **Draft:** In my free time, I like to read books. Sometimes I play soccer with my friends. I enjoy listening to music. I also like cooking.
>
> **Revision note:** I keep the message short, use familiar words, and finish with one clear next step. Other well-supported responses are possible.

### Why the model works

The model shows one defensible route through the task; it is not a script to memorize. Compare its task completion, language accuracy, audience fit, and clarity with your response, then revise once.

### Transfer task

Invite the friend to join one activity this weekend. Keep the target language, but change the details and register needed for the new context.
