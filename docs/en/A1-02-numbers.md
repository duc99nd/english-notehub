# Numbers & Phone Numbers

## Why numbers first

After "What's your name?", the next question is almost always a number: phone, age, address, price, time. Get comfortable with numbers and a hundred daily situations get easier.

## Counting 1 to 20

Most of the numbers from 1 to 12 are **unique words** — you just memorize them. From 13 onwards, a clear pattern starts.

| 1–10    | 11–20                        |
| ------- | ---------------------------- |
| 1 one   | 11 eleven                    |
| 2 two   | 12 twelve                    |
| 3 three | 13 thirteen (= three + teen) |
| 4 four  | 14 fourteen (= four + teen)  |
| 5 five  | 15 fifteen (= five + teen)   |
| 6 six   | 16 sixteen                   |
| 7 seven | 17 seventeen                 |
| 8 eight | 18 eighteen                  |
| 9 nine  | 19 nineteen                  |
| 10 ten  | 20 twenty                    |

> **Pattern**: 13–19 = `[3–9] + teen`. Note: **five** + teen = **fifteen** (drop the "v"), and **eight** + teen = **eighteen** (drop the "t").

## Tens from 20 to 100

From 20 onwards, the pattern is `[number] + ty`.

| Number | Spelling    | Note                                |
| ------ | ----------- | ----------------------------------- |
| 20     | twenty      | base form                           |
| 30     | thirty      | drop the "e" in three               |
| 40     | **forty**   | not "fourty" — common spelling trap |
| 50     | **fifty**   | not "fivety" — drop the "e" in five |
| 60     | sixty       | drop the "x" in six                 |
| 70     | seventy     | keep the "v" in seven               |
| 80     | eighty      | drop one "t" in eight               |
| 90     | ninety      | drop the "e" in nine                |
| 100    | one hundred | "a" or "one" both work              |

> **Tip**: Twenty, thirty, forty, fifty, sixty, seventy, eighty, ninety, hundred — practice saying them in order. The rhythm (two syllables, stress on the first) is the same every time.

## Compound numbers 21–99

For 21 to 99, use a hyphen: `[tens] + [-] + [unit]`.

| Number | Spoken                                              |
| ------ | --------------------------------------------------- |
| 21     | twenty-one                                          |
| 35     | thirty-five                                         |
| 48     | forty-eight                                         |
| 67     | sixty-seven                                         |
| 99     | ninety-nine                                         |
| 100    | one hundred                                         |
| 101    | one hundred and one                                 |
| 250    | two hundred fifty (or "two fifty" in casual speech) |

> **Pattern**: For 101+, English uses **"and"** before the last digits. "One hundred **and** five" = 105. (American English sometimes drops the "and", but in writing always include it.)

## Saying phone numbers

> **Pattern**: read **each digit** separately, with a small pause every 3-4 digits.
>
> `0 = oh` (or "zero"), `1 = one`, `2 = two`, ..., `9 = nine`

| Written      | How you say it                                        |
| ------------ | ----------------------------------------------------- |
| 091 234 5678 | "Zero nine one, two three four, five six seven eight" |
| 098 765 4321 | "Oh nine eight, seven six five, four three two one"   |
| 1900 1530    | "One nine zero zero, one five three zero"             |

Two important habits:

1. **Group digits into 3s and 4s.** Saying all 10 digits at once confuses listeners.
2. **Use "oh" for 0 in phone numbers.** "Zero" is correct too, but "oh" is faster and matches what you hear when someone dictates a number on the phone.

## Simple math

> **Pattern**:
>
> - `X plus Y` = cộng
> - `X minus Y` = trừ
> - `X times Y` = nhân
> - `X divided by Y` = chia
> - `X equals Y` = bằng

| Spoken                               | Meaning    |
| ------------------------------------ | ---------- |
| One plus two equals three.           | 1 + 2 = 3  |
| Ten minus four equals six.           | 10 − 4 = 6 |
| Three times five equals fifteen.     | 3 × 5 = 15 |
| Twelve divided by four equals three. | 12 ÷ 4 = 3 |

## Listen to a real phone-call introduction

> **Receptionist**: Good morning, Sunflower Café. May I have your name, please?
>
> **Customer**: Hi. My name is Duc.
>
> **Receptionist**: Thanks, Duc. And your phone number?
>
> **Customer**: It's zero nine one, two three four, five six seven eight.
>
> **Receptionist**: Zero nine one, two three four, five six seven eight. Got it. And how many people?
>
> **Customer**: Four people, please.
>
> **Receptionist**: Four people. What day would you like to book?
>
> **Customer**: This Saturday, the sixteenth of November.

Notice how the receptionist **repeats the number back** to confirm. This is the standard move when receiving a number over the phone — always repeat it.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ "Fourty" — correct is **forty** (no "u").
- ❌ "Fiveteen" — correct is **fifteen** (no "v").
- ❌ "My number is oh-nine-one-two-three-four-five-six-seven-eight-eight" — too many digits at once. Pause every 3-4.
- ❌ "Hundred and five" (for 1005 — actually that's right!) — wait, 1005 = "one thousand and five". "One hundred and five" = 105.

## Culture note: dates and prices

Americans say **"April fifth"** or **"the fifth of April"** for 4/5, while Vietnamese often says "mùng 5 tháng 4". In English, **the month comes first** when you write the date: `04/05/2026 = April 5, 2026`.

For prices: "$3.50" = "**three fifty**" or "**three dollars and fifty cents**". In casual speech, drop the cents if it's obvious: "$50" = "fifty bucks" (slang) or "fifty dollars".
