# Time & Places

## Why time + place matter

Every English sentence about real life needs a time and a place. "I eat." — when? where? Add "I eat **lunch at noon at the office**" and you have a real, specific statement. Twenty time words and ten place words let you describe 90% of daily situations.

## Times of day

| English       | Vietnamese     | Roughly      |
| ------------- | -------------- | ------------ |
| morning       | buổi sáng      | 6 AM – 12 PM |
| noon / midday | buổi trưa      | 12 PM        |
| afternoon     | buổi chiều     | 12 PM – 6 PM |
| evening       | buổi tối (sớm) | 6 PM – 10 PM |
| night         | ban đêm        | 10 PM – 6 AM |
| midnight      | nửa đêm        | 12 AM        |

> **Tip**: "Evening" and "night" overlap. In casual speech, anything after dark is "evening" until you go to bed; after that, it's "night". A 7 PM dinner is "evening"; a 2 AM party is "night".

## Days of the week

| English   | Vietnamese | Short | Pronunciation tip |
| --------- | ---------- | ----- | ----------------- |
| Monday    | thứ Hai    | Mon   | "Mun-day"         |
| Tuesday   | thứ Ba     | Tue   | "Tyooz-day"       |
| Wednesday | thứ Tư     | Wed   | "Wenz-day"        |
| Thursday  | thứ Năm    | Thu   | "Thurz-day"       |
| Friday    | thứ Sáu    | Fri   | "Fry-day"         |
| Saturday  | thứ Bảy    | Sat   | "Sat-ur-day"      |
| Sunday    | Chủ Nhật   | Sun   | "Sun-day"         |

> **Trick**: The "-day" pronunciation is the hard part for Vietnamese speakers. "Wednesday" is **ONE syllable plus -day**: /wenz-day/, NOT "Wed-nes-day". Same for "Saturday", "Tuesday", "Thursday".

## The three prepositions: at, in, on

This is one of the first grammar decisions every learner makes — and it's confusing because the same word can change meaning.

> **Pattern A**: `at [specific time]`
>
> - `at 7 o'clock`, `at noon`, `at night`, `at midnight`
>
> **Pattern B**: `in [period of time]`
>
> - `in the morning`, `in the afternoon`, `in May`, `in 2026`
>
> **Pattern C**: `on [specific day / date]`
>
> - `on Monday`, `on Sunday`, `on July 4th`, `on my birthday`

### The "at home" exception

When the place is "home", "work", or "school" (a small set of everyday places where you "are"), use **at** — not "in":

| English                | Why                                          |
| ---------------------- | -------------------------------------------- |
| `I am at home.`        | (home is where you live, treated as a point) |
| `I am at work.`        | (work is your workplace)                     |
| `I am at school.`      | (school is where you study)                  |
| `I am in the office.`  | (a specific building, use "in")              |
| `I am in the library.` | (a room inside a building, use "in")         |

> **Quick rule**: "at" for **places-as-activities** (home, work, school), "in" for **places-as-spaces** (the office, the library, Vietnam).

## Listen to a real weekend conversation

> **Anna**: What do you usually do on Saturday?
>
> **Duc**: I usually go to the market in the morning. My wife likes fresh vegetables.
>
> **Anna**: Smart. What about in the afternoon?
>
> **Duc**: I take my son to the park. We play football. Sometimes we go to the coffee shop on Nguyen Hue street.
>
> **Anna**: That sounds nice. Do you work on Sunday?
>
> **Duc**: Never. Sunday is for family. In the morning, we go to my parents' house. In the afternoon, I read at home or visit a friend.
>
> **Anna**: I love Sundays. In the evening, I usually stay at home and watch a movie.
>
> **Duc**: Same here. By the way, when is the meeting? On Monday or Tuesday?
>
> **Anna**: On Monday morning, at 9 o'clock, at the office.
>
> **Duc**: Got it. See you on Monday at 9.

Notice how Duc uses **on + day**, **in + part of day**, and **at + specific time** consistently. Each one triggers the right preposition automatically.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ "I work **at** Monday." — wrong preposition for day. ✅ "I work **on** Monday."
- ❌ "I wake up **in** six o'clock." — wrong preposition for time. ✅ "I wake up **at** six o'clock."
- ❌ "I am **on** home." — wrong preposition for home. ✅ "I am **at** home."
- ❌ "I study **at** the morning." — wrong preposition for part of day. ✅ "I study **in** the morning."
- ❌ "She works on a hospital." — wrong. ✅ "She works **at** a hospital."

## Culture note: schedules and calendars

**The work week in English-speaking countries** is **Monday to Friday**, 9 AM to 5 PM (or 9 to 6). **Saturday and Sunday are the weekend**. "I work weekdays" means Mon-Fri.

**Vietnamese work week** is also Monday to Friday, but many offices are open Saturday morning. "I work half-day Saturday" is a very common phrase in Vietnamese that English speakers sometimes translate directly — but in English, just say "I work **on Saturday morning**" or "I work **Saturday mornings**".

**Months**: January, February, March, April, May, June, July, August, September, October, November, December. (No accents or diacritics in English.) The abbreviation is the first three letters: Jan, Feb, Mar, etc.
