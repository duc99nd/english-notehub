# Past Events

## Past Simple Tense

We use the **past simple** to talk about completed actions in the past.

> **Pattern**: `Subject + verb (past) + time expression`

## Regular verbs

Add **-ed** to the base verb.

| Base | Past   | Example                  |
| ---- | ------ | ------------------------ |
| work | worked | I worked late yesterday. |
| play | played | They played football.    |
| walk | walked | She walked to school.    |

## Irregular verbs

Irregular verbs change their form. You need to memorize them.

| Base | Past   | Example                      |
| ---- | ------ | ---------------------------- |
| go   | went   | I went to the cinema.        |
| eat  | ate    | She ate pizza for lunch.     |
| see  | saw    | They saw a beautiful sunset. |
| take | took   | He took a photo.             |
| buy  | bought | I bought a new book.         |

## Was and Were

Use **was** with I, he, she, it. Use **were** with you, we, they.

| Subject        | Verb | Example                  |
| -------------- | ---- | ------------------------ |
| I, He, She, It | was  | She was happy yesterday. |
| You, We, They  | were | They were at home.       |

## Negative and questions

- `I did not (didn't) go.` → Did you go?
- `She was tired.` → Was she tired?

### Notice the language

Read the situation, then listen to the model once for meaning and once for phrasing.

> **Colleague:** How would you handle **past events** in this situation?
>
> **You:** I went to the market yesterday.

Notice what the response does before you copy its wording: it gives the listener a clear point, enough context, and a usable next step.

> **Pattern**: clear point + concrete detail or reason + appropriate next step

### Your turn

### Model response

The model is one possible response, not a script you must memorize. Compare its purpose, level of detail, and tone with your version, then revise once.
