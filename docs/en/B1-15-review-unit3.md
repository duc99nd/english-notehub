# Review Unit 3

## What We Learned

In Unit 3, you learned four important skills for everyday life:

1. **Food & Cooking** - Vocabulary for ingredients, cooking methods, and meals
2. **First & Second Conditionals** - Talking about real and imaginary situations
3. **Recipes & Menus** - Reading and understanding recipes and menus
4. **Blog Post or Review** - Writing reviews and sharing opinions

## Quick Review

### Food & Cooking Vocabulary

| Category | Examples                                     |
| -------- | -------------------------------------------- |
| Methods  | to boil, to fry, to bake, to steam, to grill |
| Actions  | to chop, to slice, to peel, to mix, to stir  |
| Meals    | breakfast, lunch, dinner, appetizer, dessert |

### Conditionals

| Type               | Structure                 | Use                        |
| ------------------ | ------------------------- | -------------------------- |
| First Conditional  | If + present, will + verb | Real and likely situations |
| Second Conditional | If + past, would + verb   | Imaginary situations       |

**Examples:**

- First: If it **rains**, I **will stay** home.
- Second: If I **won** the lottery, I **would travel**.

### Review Structure

1. Introduction: What you are reviewing
2. Body: Details and experiences
3. Conclusion: Recommendation (Overall, I would recommend...)

## Learning outcome

By the end of this lesson, you can combine language from the unit to resolve a realistic task involving review unit 3 and justify one revision.

## Scenario practice

### Situation

Plan a fictional team meal: explain a dish, use a conditional for one risk, read a menu, and publish a short recommendation.

> **Training note:** This situation and its details are fictional. They are designed for language practice, not presented as a real event, policy, study, or legal interpretation.

### Controlled practice

Select language from at least three lessons in this unit. For each choice, note the job it performs: describe, connect, qualify, request, or conclude.

### Guided task

Write 45–135 words to complete the integrated task. Use material from at least three lessons, then annotate one revision that improved accuracy, tone, or clarity.

### Model response

> **Integrated response:** Let's review what we learned in Unit 3. You can now talk about food and cooking. You understand first and second conditionals.
>
> **Revision note:** I organize the response so the situation comes first, the relevant detail follows, and the final sentence makes the requested action clear. I would revise any sentence that is correct but difficult for the audience to process. Other well-supported responses are possible.

### Why the model works

The model shows one defensible route through the task; it is not a script to memorize. Compare its task completion, language accuracy, audience fit, and clarity with your response, then revise once.

### Transfer task

Change the event from dinner to a morning workshop. Keep the target language, but change the details and register needed for the new context.
