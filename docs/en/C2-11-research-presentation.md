# Research Presentation Vocabulary

## The Rhetoric of Scholarly Defense

Research presentations, particularly doctoral defenses, occupy a unique register: you must project **confidence in findings** while maintaining appropriate **epistemic humility**. This balance distinguishes sophisticated scholarship.

### Methodological Vocabulary

#### Research Design

> **Pattern**: `This study employs a [mixed-methods/qualitative/quantitative] design, integrating [method 1] with [method 2] through methodological triangulation.`

> **Pattern**: `The research adopted a [cross-sectional/longitudinal/case study] approach, enabling examination of [specific phenomenon].`

#### Data Collection and Analysis

| Term             | Definition                          | Usage Context        |
| ---------------- | ----------------------------------- | -------------------- |
| Triangulation    | Using multiple methods/data sources | Validity enhancement |
| Saturation       | No new themes emerging              | Qualitative rigor    |
| Generalizability | Applicability beyond sample         | Quantitative claims  |
| Transferability  | Applicability to other contexts     | Qualitative claims   |

### Defending Your Methodology

#### Anticipating Challenges

> **Pattern**: `The decision to employ [method] was informed by [consideration 1] and [consideration 2]; critics may note [potential limitation], which I address in [section].`

> **Pattern**: `While a [alternative method] approach might yield [different finding], the present study prioritizes [specific aspect] over [trade-off].`

#### Defending Limitations

> **Pattern**: `Notwithstanding the limitations inherent in [method/constraints], the findings remain robust because [justification].`

> **Pattern**: `The generalizability of these findings is necessarily constrained by [factor]; however, the depth of analysis compensates by [benefit].`

## Presenting Findings

### Claim Strength and Hedging

| Assertion type    | Hedging required | Example                             |
| ----------------- | ---------------- | ----------------------------------- |
| Established fact  | None             | "The data demonstrate..."           |
| Strong finding    | Minimal          | "The evidence strongly suggests..." |
| Tentative finding | Moderate         | "The findings may indicate..."      |
| Speculation       | Strong           | "One might extrapolate that..."     |

### Statistical Reporting

> **Pattern**: `The correlation between X and Y was statistically significant (r = [value], p < [threshold]), accounting for [percentage] of the variance.`

> **Pattern**: `The effect size (Cohen's d = [value]) suggests a [small/medium/large] practical significance beyond statistical significance.`

## Responding to the Committee

### Handling Tough Questions

> **Pattern**: `That's an astute observation. The short answer is [X]; the longer answer requires acknowledging [nuance].`

> **Pattern**: `I would qualify the finding by noting that [limitation], which leads to the revised interpretation that [revised claim].`

> **Pattern**: `The committee member raises a valid concern about [issue]; my response would be threefold: first [X], second [Y], and third [Z].`

### Defending Against Critique

> **Pattern**: `I am indebted to the committee for that observation—it touches on a genuine tension in the literature between [view A] and [view B].`

> **Pattern**: `I would argue that the apparent contradiction dissolves when we distinguish between [concept A] and [concept B].`
